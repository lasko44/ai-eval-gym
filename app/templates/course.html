The job this targets

A hands-on staff or senior engineer on a fintech AI platform team, not a director. Three real postings set the surface. One asks its staff engineer to own the evaluation and feedback loop. That means offline evals, tracing, and regression gates in continuous integration, with production failures and user edits turning into new eval cases. The same posting lists eval dataset design, tracing, judge scoring and regression testing as required skills.

A second wants evaluation frameworks that measure correctness, reliability and safety for non-deterministic systems at scale. A third owns an eval platform alongside the cost telemetry that says what the answers are worth.

The six parts below cover that surface, and each part says which piece of it the part answers. The same postings ask for least-privilege access to enterprise data, agent identity and audit logging, which is what part 6 is.

One honest thing. These roles ask for production experience with language models, and no course supplies that. A course can do one thing instead. When somebody asks how you would measure a thing, you have already measured it, on a system shaped like theirs. You can also say what the number did not tell you. The gap here is reps, not years, and reps are the one thing you can close on your own.

How a unit works

  1. Read what goes wrong, with the measured numbers and the primary sources.
  2. Predict three questions, each with how sure you are, before any answer appears. Being confidently wrong costs you here the way it does in a real teardown.
  3. Detect by writing the measurement yourself against a fixture in this repo, then posting the number. The app checks it against the fixture's known result. It does not run your code.
  4. Review what you got right, where your confidence was off, and where the numbers came from.

What the scoreboard counts, and what it refuses to

  • Only your first answer to a probe counts. A probe answered after you have read the explanation is not a prediction, so counting it would reward rereading and call that learning. Answer again as often as you like on Review. It cannot move the number in either direction.
  • Mastering a unit does not require being right. It requires committing every prediction and posting a detection that matched. If correctness were the bar, the clever move would be to pick the safest-looking option rather than the one you believe. That destroys the only thing a probe is for. Being wrong is paid for in calibration instead.
  • Rank and calibration are never added together. Rank comes from parts mastered. Calibration stands on its own and can be poor while your rank is high. That is a state worth seeing rather than one worth hiding inside an average.
  • A part stays locked until you finish the one before it. Every part and every unit claims what it builds on, and the lock is what makes that claim true rather than decorative. Any locked unit still opens if you insist.

After the units

A unit gives you a fixture with a known answer. That is how you learn a measurement without also arguing about whether you got it right. A cycle points the same skill at a real system that nobody has graded. You commit what you think is wrong with it, seal that prediction, then measure and find out. That is the part you can show somebody.
