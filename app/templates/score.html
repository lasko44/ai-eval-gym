Reading it

The Brier score is the mean of (probability - outcome) squared over the claims that resolved: 0.000 is perfect calibration and lower is better. Saying 0.5 to everything scores 0.250, so that is the number to beat before your confidence is carrying any information.

1.000 is the worst possible, and it is earned in either direction. A probability of 1.0 on a claim that resolved wrong earns it, and so does a probability of 0.0 on a claim that resolved correct. The second shape is a confident denial of a problem that turned out to be real.

Coverage is resolved claims over all claims on this cycle. A claim whose metric no harness run measured is unresolved: it is counted here, and left out of the Brier score entirely, so a low coverage means the score rests on less than you predicted.
