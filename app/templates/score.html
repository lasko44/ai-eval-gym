Reading it

The Brier score is the mean of (probability - outcome) squared over the claims that resolved: 0.000 is perfect calibration and lower is better. Saying 0.5 to everything scores 0.250, so that is the number to beat before your confidence is carrying any information.

1.000 is the worst possible score. You earn it in either direction. A probability of 1.0 on a claim that resolved wrong earns it, and so does a probability of 0.0 on a claim that resolved correct. The second shape is a confident denial of a problem that turned out to be real.

Coverage is the share of claims that resolved. A claim stays unresolved when no harness run measured its metric. Coverage counts that claim. The Brier score leaves it out. So low coverage means the score rests on less than you predicted.
